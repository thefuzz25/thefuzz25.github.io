### How the read went

Not half bad. I don’t normally read books without a background on either the author or the story, but I liked this one. Blake Crouch makes a fairly complicated premise accessible without drowning it in scientific jargon. The story moves quickly, and there’s always another question waiting around the corner to keep you going.

What makes the book particularly interesting is how it uses science fiction as a vehicle for a rather personal dilemma. Beneath all the alternate realities and scientific explanations, there’s a fairly straightforward question driving the story: what if the life you had chosen wasn’t the only one you could have lived?

### What sits beneath the story

At its core, _Dark Matter_ is about the lives we build and the lives we leave behind. We make choices, settle into routines, and eventually begin to treat the path we’ve taken as the only one that could have worked out. Crouch takes that assumption and turns it on its head.

The idea of alternate realities is fascinating in itself, but what gives the story its emotional weight is the relationship between possibility and contentment. Would having access to every conceivable version of your life make you happier, or would it simply make you more aware of everything you’ve missed?

There’s also something unsettling about the thought that the person you are is, at least in part, a product of circumstance. Change a few decisions, put yourself in a different environment, and perhaps you become someone else entirely. The question isn’t merely whether another version of you exists, but whether you’d recognise yourself in them.

And somewhere along the way, the book makes you reconsider the value of the life you already have. Not because it’s necessarily the best one imaginable, but because it’s the one you’ve actually lived.

### Should you read it?

If you’re looking for a fast-paced science-fiction thriller that doesn’t require you to be particularly well-versed in science, this is a solid pick. The premise is compelling, and the ideas beneath it give you something to think about after the book is done.

Not a bad birthday present, little sis.
