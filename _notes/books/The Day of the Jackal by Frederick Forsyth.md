### Why I picked it up

Maybe because,
- I wanted my next one to be a filler read before gearing up for a thicker novel.[^1]
- Started watching it's tv adaptation featuring Eddie Redmayne, and the story was compelling.

### How the read went

The role assigned to this book was to help me survive college lectures. And that I did.

The magnitude of events that led to the introduction of the Jackal was easy to follow, and Forsyth does a good job of establishing the political circumstances before getting into the actual assassination plot. You understand why the operation exists, who’s involved, and what’s at stake. No need to spend half the book wondering what everyone is doing or why.

What follows is essentially a race against time, told from two sides. On one, you have a professional assassin meticulously preparing for his assignment. On the other, you have the authorities attempting to identify him before he can carry it out.

The interesting part is just how much attention Forsyth gives to the preparation itself. The disguises, false identities, weapons, documents, and logistics might sound mundane on paper, but they become the very things that keep the plot moving. It’s a thriller where the small details matter, and the tension comes from watching a carefully constructed plan take shape while the authorities slowly close in.

### What sits beneath the story

What makes _The Day of the Jackal_ work is its treatment of professionalism. The Jackal isn’t presented as someone who relies on luck or spectacular displays of violence. He’s methodical, patient, and exceptionally good at what he does. His anonymity is as important as his skill.

That makes him a rather unusual antagonist. You don’t need to agree with his motives to appreciate the precision with which he’s written. In fact, much of the suspense comes from watching someone so competent operate against a system that has to contend with bureaucracy, competing interests, and incomplete information.

The novel also has an interesting relationship with time. The reader knows what the Jackal intends to do, but the authorities don’t know who he is. The suspense, therefore, isn’t simply about whether the assassination will happen. It’s about whether the investigation can uncover enough information, quickly enough, to stop it.

And that’s where Forsyth’s attention to detail pays off. The plot feels less like a sequence of convenient twists and more like a contest between preparation and discovery.

### Should you read it?

If you’re in the mood for a political thriller that prioritises planning, procedure, and suspense over constant action, this is worth picking up. It does demand some patience, particularly when the narrative gets into the finer details of the operation, but those details are also what make the eventual payoff work.

And if you’re looking for something to keep you occupied through a few college lectures, it certainly serves that purpose.[^2]
