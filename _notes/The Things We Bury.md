date: 2026-10-24

---
I found this poetry prompt online the other day: 

> Write a poem with one repeated line appearing five times. Each time the line returns, the surrounding context must alter its meaning through irony, tenderness or revelation.

Like most things online, I read it, thought '*that’s interesting,*' and scrolled on. But today I came across the Arabic phrase ==“Ya’aburnee”== which can loosely be translated as “you bury me.” It means love so consuming it wishes to die first, just to be spared the grief of the other’s absence. 

The phrase stayed with me, and then it began changing shape. Below is my attempt at that prompt. An attempt[^1] that became a current I hadn’t quite meant to follow.

---

### Ya'aburnee

**I. The things we told the dark**

There was a season of open windows,  
of midnight spilling into morning,  
of words arriving faster  
than either of us could think to stop them.

We had met by accident,  
two names on the same roster,  
two small disasters  
sharing the weather.

And what a curious thing,  
to find a stranger already fluent  
in the language of things going wrong.

We emptied ourselves in handfuls,  
laid our little ruins on the table,  
held up the broken pieces  
as though naming them  
might keep them from breaking further.

Outside, the world went on  
with its ordinary cruelties.  
Inside, there was always another message,  
another hour borrowed from sleep,  
another confession met with  
*and another thing:*

We did not know each other.  
We knew where it hurt.

For a while,  
that was enough.

You bury me.

And I mistook the earth above me  
for the hands that would pull me out.

***

**II. The map**

It is strange what the light reveals.

We had always been made  
of incompatible weather,  
though the storms, at first,  
were elsewhere.

We spread the same map between us,  
traced the same rivers with our fingers,  
and arrived, somehow,  
at opposite ends of the world.

For a while, we called this interesting.  
Later, we began to call it something else.

The roads grew narrower.  
Every turn became a question  
of whose compass had failed,  
whose north was merely  
another kind of south.

We pointed at the same landmarks,  
each certain the other  
was looking at them wrong.

Neither of us was lost, perhaps.  
We had only begun to mistake  
our own directions for the destination.

You bury me.

And somewhere between  
where we had been  
and where we thought we were going,  
the map tore along its folds.

***

**III. The architecture of silence**

Eventually, we learned the house.

Which doors swelled in the rain.  
Which floorboards complained.  
Which rooms were best left dark.

We learned the art of leaving  
a sentence unfinished,  
of letting a question pass  
without examining what it might cost us.

There was a kind of peace in this.

The cups remained unbroken.  
The windows stayed shut.  
We could sit through an entire evening  
without discovering a new reason  
to misunderstand each other.

And sometimes,  
we even forgot to be careful.

A laugh escaped.  
A story ran too long.  
For a moment, the old house  
filled with the noise of its former inhabitants.

Then someone remembered  
where the walls were.

We had not stopped caring.  
We had simply grown familiar  
with the dimensions of the damage.

You bury me.

And I learned that a house may stand  
for years, its doors still opening,  
though certain rooms  
have forgotten the way to one another.

***

**IV. What the earth cannot keep**

Then came the things  
for which we had no language.

Whatever broke in you  
did not resemble what broke in me.  
We stood on opposite banks  
of a river neither had chosen,  
holding out our hands  
as though distance were a problem  
of measurement.

I offered what had once saved me.  
You offered what had once saved you.

Neither offering survived the crossing.

There are griefs that refuse instruction,  
wounds that turn away the light,  
and certain nights  
that do not become morning  
so much as something else.

We had mistaken knowing  
for the beginning of an answer.

We had mistaken staying  
for the ability to help.

Now there was only the earth,  
and all the things it takes  
without making them whole.

You bury me.

The shovel grows heavy.  
The ground gives nothing back.

***

**V. A return to the surface**

I have been thinking about the boy  
who needed no one.

Or thought he didn't.

He kept his own counsel,  
carried his own weather,  
knew the exact weight of his silence  
and called it strength.

He had made a life  
of never handing anyone  
anything sharp enough  
to cut him with.

Then, somewhere along the way,  
he began leaving the door unlocked.

Now he stands in the garden  
after everyone has gone,  
looking at the disturbed earth,  
the little mounds where things once grew,  
the places where the grass  
has not yet learned to return.

He could fill them in.  
Flatten the ground.  
Let the rain do the rest.

He could tell himself  
that nothing had been lost,  
that the roots had only grown too deep,  
that the garden was never his to keep.

Perhaps tomorrow  
he will begin.

Perhaps he will leave it as it is.

You bury me.

And I cannot tell  
whether I am the one beneath the earth  
or the one who has come  
to cover it.

---

[^1]: Some things take longer to write because they take longer to understand. This is one of those.
